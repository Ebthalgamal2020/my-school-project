Everything in one calm view

Three things most planners get wrong, and how Meridian handles them instead.

  • Deadlines that find you

    Paste a syllabus, get a timeline. Every due date lands on a calendar you can actually read at a glance.

  • Workload you can see

    Spot the week where three finals collide — while there's still time to start early instead of all at once.

  • Focus blocks, not guilt

    Meridian suggests short, realistic study sessions that fit the gaps you already have between classes.

Three steps, once a semester

Set it up in the first week and coast on it until finals.

  1. 01

    Add your classes

    Name, meeting times, and the syllabus. That's the whole setup.

  2. 02

    Review the plan

    Meridian drafts your semester. Drag anything that looks wrong.

  3. 03

    Check in weekly

    Five minutes each Sunday keeps the whole term on track.

Why I built this

I kept missing assignments that I knew about — they just lived in six different places. Meridian is my attempt at the planner I wanted: one screen, no notifications, no streaks to maintain.

It's built as a course project with plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript — no frameworks, no build step. The source is on GitHub if you'd like to poke at it.
